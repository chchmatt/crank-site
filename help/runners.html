Runners

A runner is one Claude Code builder, working on one topic's job, on your computer. The controller starts and ends runners. You can end them too.

Going offline, sleep and power loss

  1. The controller sends a heartbeat every 30 seconds, building or idle. Why: without one, the page can't tell a quiet build from a dead computer.
  2. After 4 missed heartbeats (about 2 minutes), the page shows "Lost contact with the controller since HH:MM", and each Building job shows "Building (last heard HH:MM)" in a muted style. It never shows plain Building past that. The number of beats is a project setting.
  3. Offline means Claude can't be reached on 4 checks in a row (about 2 minutes). Short blips are ridden out; Claude Code retries by itself.
  4. When the computer goes offline, no new runner starts. A runner that's building is ended (Claude can't work offline anyway). Its unsaved work is saved on its branch. The job goes back Queued at the front of its topic, with the note "Interrupted: offline at HH:MM".
  5. It resumes by itself. When the computer has been back online for a minute, the controller sends what it kept, starts runners, and picks the interrupted job first. The runner carries on from the job's branch.
  6. Sleep or power loss is treated the same: on start, the controller finds runners that are gone and puts their jobs back Queued at the front, with the note "Interrupted: the computer slept or lost power".
  7. Why offline gives Queued but End task gives Paused: going offline wasn't your choice, so Crank carries on when it can. Ending a task was your choice, so Crank waits for you.
  8. While a job is building, the desktop app keeps the computer from going to sleep (a closed laptop lid can still sleep; rule 6 covers that). An optional setting ends runners after their current jobs when on battery, and starts them again on mains power.

Source: CB-44, CB-46.

When a runner needs you

  1. A runner that needs you asks one question instead of stopping: approve or reject, a short text answer, or a step to do at the computer with a Done button.
  2. The job goes Needs operator. You get one phone alert ("CB-12: Needs operator: <the question>"), and the question shows at the top of the status page. If you haven't answered within an hour, it's said once more, then no more.
  3. Other topics keep building while one waits for you.
  4. When you answer, the job goes back to the front of its topic and the runner carries on from that step. Reject stops the job at that step, with the reason.
  5. A question about money, DNS, accounts or deleting something is always an approve question. Nothing secret goes in a question or an answer, and your text answer is treated as information for the runner, never as a command.
  6. Alerts go to your phone only: no pop-ups or Windows notifications on the computer. Why: the computer stays free for you to use.
  7. Azure steps work the same way: a change that isn't a free, add-only one is approved from the phone, and an expired Azure sign-in sends its code only to your phone alert.

Source: CB-12, CB-13, CB-22.

Which Claude model runners use

Runners use Sonnet 5.5 by default. When the Fable weekly limit is past your threshold, runs use Sonnet 5.5 (see Usage and schedules).

Source: CB-67.

VS Code (attended jobs)

Some jobs need a person at the computer, such as an Azure, DNS or GitHub step or a live push. These are marked Attended and are built in VS Code when you type todo. todo names the job it would take and why, and asks "Take job CB-12? (yes / no / a job number)" before starting anything. todo CB-12 takes that job, or says in one line why it can't.

Source: CB-14.
