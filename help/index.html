How Crank works

Prompts in. Tested code merged.

Crank is a build queue for AI builders. You write each piece of work as a job (a prompt), Crank's runners build the jobs on your own computer with Claude Code, test them, and merge the finished work into your code. You watch and steer it all from the status page at crankbuild.com.

These pages explain what Crank does and why, in plain words. You don't need to know git to read them. Each rule is stated once, in the page where it belongs, with the reason for it.

The pages

  1. Glossary: job, topic, runner, controller, operator and the rest.
  2. Jobs and their states: what Waiting, Queued, Built and Merged mean, what a job waits for, reordering, and split jobs.
  3. Topics: lines of work, pausing and removing them, and why a topic has no runner.
  4. Runners: ending tasks, going offline, sleep, memory limits, and questions a runner asks you.
  5. Claude usage and schedules: the usage threshold, and starting work only at certain times.
  6. Merging: when a topic merges, merge rules, Hold and Release, base branches like version-2.
  7. Branches and GitHub: what Crank pushes, what it deletes and when, squash, and what happens on a clash.
  8. Controllers, the API and your privacy: one controller per project, taking over, the desktop app, and what never leaves your computer.
  9. The status page: The list, the job page, estimates, live updates and alerts.
  10. Questions people ask: short answers that point to the rule, for example "Why was the CB-123 branch deleted?"

About these pages

  • They describe the rules agreed on 25 November 2026. Many are being built now. Each section ends with a Source line naming the Crank job (for example CB-79) that builds it. Until that job is Merged, the rule describes how Crank will behave.
  • They are written to be the source for Crank's help chat, so each answer can quote one rule and its reason. When a rule changes, change it here, in one place.
  • Times on the page are in your computer's time zone, 24-hour, with the weekday when it isn't today (for example "Sun 10:00").
