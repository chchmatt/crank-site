Jobs and their states

A job is one prompt: one piece of work, written so a builder can do it alone. This page covers what a job's state means, what it waits for, how the order is set, and what happens to work that can't be finished yet.

Every job has exactly one state Coming soon (CB-32)

StateWhat it means
WaitingIt waits for another job that isn't finished yet.
QueuedIt can start as soon as its topic has a free runner.
PausedIt won't start until someone resumes it.
BuildingA runner is working on it now.
Needs operatorIt's stopped until you answer, approve or fix something. The reason is in its note.
BuiltIts work is finished, tested and in its topic, but the topic hasn't merged into its base branch yet.
MergedIts topic has merged into its base branch (usually main), with this job in it.
CancelledIt won't be built.

Rules:

  1. One state, never two. Why: a job that looked both "Ready" and "Held" left people guessing what would happen next.
  2. A job only counts as done when it's Merged. Built isn't done: the work isn't in your main code yet. Why: "done" should mean you can use it.
  3. Merged is kept for good, with when it merged, its topic, and the commit. When the base isn't main, it says so, for example "Merged into version-2".
  4. A failed merge isn't a job state. The jobs stay Built and the topic gets a note, for example "merge blocked, fix job CB-29". The note goes away when the merge succeeds. Why: the jobs themselves are fine; it's the topic's merge that needs fixing.
  5. Hold all is a banner, not a state. It shows above The list.

Source: CB-31, CB-32.

Pausing a job Coming soon (CB-44, CB-61, CB-62)

  1. You can pause a Waiting or Queued job, and resume a Paused one, from The list or the job's page.
  2. A job that's Building can't be paused. Use the runner's End task or End after instead (see Runners).
  3. Pausing a job doesn't pause the jobs that wait for it. They stay Waiting, with the note "after CB-12, paused". Why: they haven't been paused themselves; they're waiting for something that has.
  4. A job can also be Paused by Crank: when you end its runner's task, or when it used more memory than its cap. Crank never resumes those by itself, because something needs your eye. Jobs interrupted by going offline or by a usage limit are put back Queued instead, and resume by themselves (see Runners).

Source: CB-31, CB-44, CB-61, CB-62.

What a job waits for (After) Coming soon (CB-39, CB-70)

  1. A job can wait for other jobs. It starts only when every one of them is Built (finished and tested in its topic).
  2. Use waits only for a real code need: the job truly can't be built without the other's code. Not for "it would be nice to do this first". Why: every wait slows the queue, and order is better set by dragging.
  3. No loops. A job can never end up waiting for itself, directly or through others. The Waits for picker doesn't offer jobs that would make a loop, and Crank refuses one if it's sent anyway.
  4. A job can't be moved above a job it waits for (see Order, below).
  5. A job being built can't have its waits changed.
  6. A scheduled job doesn't make others wait, unless they list it.

Source: CB-39, CB-70; agreed 25 Nov 2026.

Order Coming soon (CB-38)

  1. The list is in build order. Each topic takes its next job from the top down.
  2. You drag a job up or down with its gripper (⋮⋮), on a PC or a phone (long-press, then drag). Up and down arrows in the row's menu do the same.
  3. Jobs stay inside their own topic's block.
  4. Drop a job above a job it waits for, and it slides to just after the last job it waits for, with a short animation and a note such as "waits for CB-31". Why: sending it back to where it started would look like nothing happened; this shows you where it can go.
  5. Needs operator and Building jobs are pinned at the top, Needs operator first and stand-out, then Building. They have no gripper and can't be dragged. A job dropped among them slides to just below the last pinned row (with no note, because nothing was refused). Why: active work should be the first thing you see. Pinning is only how the page shows it; it doesn't change the queue's order.
  6. If the list changed while you were dragging, Crank refuses the move, reloads the list and says so.

Source: CB-38.

Each job has its own page Coming soon (CB-39, CB-57, CB-71)

  1. Click a job and it opens its own page (not a pop-up) at /project/{project id}/job/{number}, with a Back link.
  2. It shows the job's id, title, state, topic, estimate, its full text, what it waits for and what waits for it, each linked.
  3. Its Waits for picker lets you choose one or more open jobs in the same project, or none. Jobs that would make a loop aren't offered.
  4. Its Start setting lets you schedule it (see Usage and schedules).
  5. Long notes in The list are cut to two lines; the full note is on the job's page. The note itself is never shortened.

Source: CB-39, CB-57, CB-71.

Unfinished work becomes a split job (no "Done in part") Coming soon (CB-37, CB-66)

  1. A job is never "Done in part". If a job finishes its own work but has steps left that it can't do yet (for example, copying a page to the website once the topic reaches main), it ends as Built, and those leftover steps become a new job.
  2. The new job's title ends "(split from CB-11)", it's in the same topic, and it waits for whatever it needs (for example, for the topic to merge).
  3. Jobs that waited for the original only wait for the split job if they truly need the leftover part.
  4. Why: "Done in part" left jobs stuck. Crank counted it as done and handed the next job to a runner, but the runner didn't, so nothing moved. A split job makes the leftover visible, ordered and buildable.

Source: CB-37, CB-66.

When a runner won't build a job Coming soon (CB-37)

If a runner ends without building a job Crank thought was free, Crank says so once, on the page and to you: "CB-12: the runner would not build it: <its reason>". It won't hand the same job out again until something changes. Why: a queue should never sit idle in silence.

Source: CB-37.

Estimates Coming soon (CB-17, CB-58)

Every job shows an estimated build time, "~N min", made from your own project's history of similar jobs, with one line of why ("like CB-4 and CB-6"). It covers the builder's own time, not the final tests and merge. See The status page.

Source: CB-17, CB-58.
