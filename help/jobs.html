Jobs and their states

A job is one prompt: one piece of work, written so a builder can do it alone. This page covers what a job's state means, what it waits for, how the order is set, and what happens to work that can't be finished yet.

Order

  1. The list is in build order. Each topic takes its next job from the top down.
  2. You drag a job up or down with its gripper (⋮⋮), on a PC or a phone (long-press, then drag). Up and down arrows in the row's menu do the same.
  3. Jobs stay inside their own topic's block.
  4. Drop a job above a job it waits for, and it slides to just after the last job it waits for, with a short animation and a note such as "waits for CB-31". Why: sending it back to where it started would look like nothing happened; this shows you where it can go.
  5. Needs operator and Building jobs are pinned at the top, Needs operator first and stand-out, then Building. They have no gripper and can't be dragged. A job dropped among them slides to just below the last pinned row (with no note, because nothing was refused). Why: active work should be the first thing you see. Pinning is only how the page shows it; it doesn't change the queue's order.
  6. If the list changed while you were dragging, Crank refuses the move, reloads the list and says so.

Source: CB-38.

Unfinished work becomes a split job (no "Done in part")

  1. A job is never "Done in part". If a job finishes its own work but has steps left that it can't do yet (for example, copying a page to the website once the topic reaches main), it ends as Built, and those leftover steps become a new job.
  2. The new job's title ends "(split from CB-11)", it's in the same topic, and it waits for whatever it needs (for example, for the topic to merge).
  3. Jobs that waited for the original only wait for the split job if they truly need the leftover part.
  4. Why: "Done in part" left jobs stuck. Crank counted it as done and handed the next job to a runner, but the runner didn't, so nothing moved. A split job makes the leftover visible, ordered and buildable.

Source: CB-37, CB-66.

When a runner won't build a job

If a runner ends without building a job Crank thought was free, Crank says so once, on the page and to you: "CB-12: the runner would not build it: <its reason>". It won't hand the same job out again until something changes. Why: a queue should never sit idle in silence.

Source: CB-37.
